\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
   The growing digitalization of healthcare services and the overload of care systems have driven the search for automated solutions for clinical triage and anamnesis. However, the direct application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in this domain faces critical challenges related to factual hallucination and the lack of structure in generated data. This work presents and technically evaluates an assisted anamnesis software system implemented as a fullstack web application. The solution adopts a hybrid Artificial Intelligence strategy, orchestrating the generative model Llama-3.1 for dialogue conduction and the extractive Biomedical-NER model for the identification and categorization of clinical entities, such as symptoms and medications. The methodology involved literature review, requirements definition, incremental development, and implementation in Next.js with relational persistence, session-based authentication, attendance management, and structured report generation. The observed results indicate that separating text generation from structured extraction reduces inconsistencies and yields outputs suitable for organizing attendances, reviewing message history, and generating clinical reports.

   \noindent 
   \textbf{Key-words}: Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare. Assisted Anamnesis. Software Engineering. LLMs. NER.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
