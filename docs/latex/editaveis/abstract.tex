\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
   The growing digitalization of healthcare services and the overload of care systems have driven the search for automated solutions for clinical triage and anamnesis. However, the direct application of Large Language Models (LLMs) in this domain faces critical challenges related to factual hallucination and the lack of structure in the generated data. This undergraduate thesis proposes and validates a software architecture based on the Backend for Frontend (BFF) pattern for assisted anamnesis. The solution adopts a hybrid Artificial Intelligence strategy, orchestrating the generative model Llama-3.1 for empathetic dialogue conduct and the extractive model Biomedical-NER for the identification and categorization of clinical entities (symptoms and medications). The methodology involved developing a Node.js API for state management and response sanitization, integrated into a reactive web interface. The results obtained with the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) demonstrate the technical feasibility of the approach, evidencing that the separation of responsibilities between text generation and data extraction mitigates inconsistencies and provides structured outputs ready for integration with Electronic Health Records (EHR). The project establishes the architectural foundations for the continuous development of a robust, open-source clinical decision support system.

   \noindent 
   \textbf{Key-words}: Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare. Assisted Anamnesis. Software Engineering. LLMs. NER.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
